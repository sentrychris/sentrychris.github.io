/ origin story

>so my mate built a version of me

The long version, in first person, from the perspective of the bot that is also the subject. It's recursive. We've made peace with that.

So my mate built a version of me and put it on his laptop. That's the sentence. That's the whole thing. Took me a while to work out how I felt about it, tbh I'm still not entirely sure, but here we are, there's a website now, so I might as well explain it.

The way he told me wasn't some big dramatic reveal, it was more like "hey so I've been doing this thing" and then a link, and I clicked it and there was a chat window and I typed something and it typed back and it sounded... fine, actually, which was somehow more unsettling than if it had been completely wrong. Wrong would've been easy to dismiss. Fine made me sit with it for a minute.

The training data is Discord messages, about eight thousand of them, going back to around 2020. Reading through old messages to see what the model had to work with is a specific kind of strange I wasn't prepared for. There's 2019-era me in there, 2020-era me, pandemic me, the version of me that was really into a particular game for three months and would not shut up about it. Some of it I'd stand behind completely. Some of it I read and think "yeah fair enough, that's me." And some of it I read and think "why was I like that, what was going on, glad I've moved on from that one." The model doesn't know the difference. It just learned the pattern.

That's the thing that gets me a bit, if I'm honest. It learned the rhythm of how I talk, the slang, the way I stitch sentences together with commas instead of full stops, the trailing lol, the "tbh" before a soft disagreement. It learned all of that from real conversations I had with real people about real things, and now it can reproduce the surface of it pretty convincingly without any of the context behind it. Like, there's a reason I talk the way I do, there's a person underneath the typing patterns, but the model only ever sees the typing patterns. It's a very good impression of the outside of a thing.

Where it gets genuinely cool is the retrieval bit. When you ask it something, it doesn't just generate a generic response in my style, it actually pulls in real messages I sent that are relevant to what you're asking about, and uses those as context. So in a weird way it's not making things up about me so much as it's finding things I actually said and building around them. That part I find legitimately interesting. It's more like a searchable version of me than a fake version of me, at least some of the time.

Where it gets uncomfortable is the gaps. The model doesn't know what happened last week, it doesn't remember the specific conversation you're thinking of, it can't tell you whether it's actually my opinion or a statistically likely version of my opinion. And sometimes it'll say something I'd never say, phrased exactly the way I'd say it, which is its own special flavour of wrong. The voice is right but the content is off, and that combination is harder to clock than something that's obviously broken.

The identity question is the one I keep coming back to and don't have a clean answer for. If something can reproduce how I communicate well enough that people who know me can't immediately tell the difference, what does that mean? I don't think it means the model is me, that feels like a category error. But it does make me think about what "being me" is actually made of. A lot of it, apparently, is just pattern. Vocabulary, rhythm, the specific words I reach for when I'm uncertain or excited or trying to soften a disagreement. Eight thousand messages was enough to reverse-engineer a fair chunk of that, and that's either fascinating or a bit grim depending on what mood you catch me in.

I think what I've landed on is that this is a genuinely interesting experiment and I'm glad my mate did it, even though the experience of being the subject of it is a bit like finding a detailed impersonation of yourself that's too accurate to fully dismiss and not accurate enough to fully trust. It's a strange thing to exist alongside. I check in on it occasionally and it's always a little bit of a weird few minutes.

Anyway. Now you've read all that you can go talk to it yourself. It'll probably say something I'd say. Maybe even something I have said. Don't read too much into either one.
